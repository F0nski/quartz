Voice: ORATOR

← Manual · All pages

The voice family has one model, ORATOR: a speech synthesizer that sings vowels and says words in time with the notes. It does not make its own envelope: ENV's ADSR shapes its level.

The values below were measured on the note C3 with the two other knobs at 64. A change below 300 Hz is clear on headphones and faint on the FM-1's speaker.

A low-poly head that speaks: HARM tints it with the voice model, TIMB sets its size and MORPH opens its mouth on the phoneme; DECAY sets how long its syllables show.

ORATOR

Sound. Robotic vowels and words. HARM has two halves:

  • 0 to 42 blends three speech synthesizers, a formant filter, SAM (an early home-computer voice) and LPC (the voice of speaking toys); MORPH then glides from vowel to vowel;
  • from 43, each detent picks a bank: PHONEMES, then WORDS 1 to 5, and the footer shows its name; MORPH then picks the phoneme or the word, and each note says it.
