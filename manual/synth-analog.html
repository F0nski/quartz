QUARTZ

Analog: CREST, SWEEP, KINK, PRISM

← Manual · All pages

The analog family is the place to start for basses, leads and plucks: simple waveforms that HARM, TIMB and MORPH bend in plain, predictable ways. None of the four makes its own envelope: ENV's ADSR shapes their level, and DECAY how long a note rings after its gate closes.

The values below were measured on the note C3 with the two other knobs at 64. A change below 300 Hz is clear on headphones and faint on the FM-1's speaker.

The scene flies over crests drawn with the model's own waveform: HARM, TIMB and MORPH reshape the crests as they reshape the sound, and DECAY sets how long the ship's shot lasts. The ship fires at the next crest on each note.

CREST

Sound. Two classic analog waveforms played together, a pulse and a saw. The plainest model of the four: a good first choice for a bass or a lead.

KnobAt 0MiddleAt 127
HARM, the second wave's pitchBoth waves on the same note: round, most of the energy on the note itselfThe second wave apart: a fuller, wider soundThe second wave higher up: the body moves up an octave and more
TIMB, the pulseA thin pulse: quieter, nasalA square: fullHard sync: a bright, cutting edge (most of the energy around 1.2–2.4 kHz)
MORPH, the sawA triangleA saw: the loudest settingA saw with a wide notch: quieter

Start with the defaults (64 everywhere), then turn TIMB: it changes the character more than the two others.

SWEEP

Sound. Classic waves through a resonant filter, as on a subtractive synth: TIMB is the filter's cutoff. The model for filter sweeps and resonant basses.

KnobAt 0MiddleAt 127
HARM, resonance and slopeA sharp resonant peak at the cutoffSmooth: no peakA sharp, louder peak, with a steeper slope
TIMB, the cutoffClosed: almost silentHalf open: the low half of the soundOpen: bright
MORPH, waveform and subOne end of the waveform and sub mixThe middle of the mixThe other end: the level barely changes, the colour does

Start with TIMB around 60 and turn it while a note plays: below 30 the sound turns dark and quiet fast.

KINK

Sound. Phase distortion, as on Casio's CZ synths: a sine bent into brighter shapes. Glassy leads and buzzy basses.

KnobAt 0MiddleAt 127
HARM, the distortion's frequencyLow: a warm, rounded toneHigher: a nasal formantHighest: bright and thin
TIMB, the amountNone: close to a sineSome: nasalFull: bright and buzzy (most of the energy around 2.4–4.8 kHz)
MORPH, asymmetrySymmetricA little bentBent: the body moves down, hollower

Start with TIMB at 30 for a clean tone, then raise it.

PRISM

Sound. A triangle shaped, then folded back on itself: from a soft sine to a metallic growl. Fold sweeps are its speciality.

KnobAt 0MiddleAt 127
HARM, the waveshapeMost of the energy around 1 kHzMuch the same, fullerSpread far up: brighter
TIMB, the foldNo fold: close to a sine, roundFolded: the body moves upFolded again: louder, harmonics far up
MORPH, asymmetrySymmetricA little bentBent: a lower, darker body

Start with TIMB at 0 for a pure tone, then fold it in.
